Study framing

Survival modeling in rare cancers is constrained by sparse target-domain data and heterogeneity across organ sites. HPB cancers are anatomically related, but whether anatomical proximity corresponds to survival-model transferability remains unclear.

Main interpretation: anatomical proximity provides a useful spatial prior for source selection under sparse target-domain data, but transferability remains partly source-target pair specific.

Low-data transfer summary

Delta C-index values are averaged across target training sizes of n = 100, 200, 300, and 500.

What this page shows

The page combines the physical distance matrix, low-data source-to-target transferability map, CT-based 3D anatomical context, and distance-rank summaries. It is designed as a static supplement to the poster rather than a server-side analytic app.

What this page does not claim

The visualizations do not imply that anatomical distance deterministically specifies transferability. They show a proximity-aligned tendency with residual pair-level variation.

Physical distance matrix

Distances were defined from organ/domain landmarks and used to assign nearest/farthest sources and source ranks.

Low-data source-target transferability map

Rows are source domains and columns are target domains. Diagonal cells are not evaluated.

3D anatomical transfer explorer

Arrows point from source to target. Line width, opacity, arrowhead size, and color reflect the low-data mean Delta C-index. CT organ surfaces are spatial context only.

Distance-rank summary

The closest anatomical source group showed the highest mean transfer gain in both endpoints, but the rank trend was not strictly monotonic.

Covariate limitations

SEER covariates were mainly demographic and staging-related; treatment and tumor biology were excluded because they were not consistently comparable across HPB domains.

This restricted covariate set may have compressed between-source differences, making the observed proximity signal a conservative estimate.

Single-source design and future extension

The single-source design allowed each source-target relationship to be evaluated separately, clarifying how anatomical proximity relates to transfer gain.

Future work should evaluate distance-weighted multi-source transfer, in which multiple anatomically related sources are combined rather than assessed independently.

Source-selection implications

Under sparse target-domain training settings, anatomically proximate organ pairs tended to show higher transfer gains.

These findings support anatomical proximity as a useful source-selection prior, while indicating that transferability remains partly source-target pair specific.

Exploratory association check

Physical distance showed a directionally consistent negative association with transfer gain, but this check should be interpreted as exploratory because pair-level variation remained and the rank-based trend was not strictly monotonic.
